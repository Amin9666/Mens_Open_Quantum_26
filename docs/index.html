01A real circuit, not a themed spreadsheet

Each Round 2 matchup gets its own quantum circuit. Three player-vs-player features go in — real ATP ranking, how dominant they were in Round 1, and how many sets it took them to get there — each encoded as a rotation angle on its own qubit. The bigger the edge for player A, the more that qubit's superposition leans toward representing "A is stronger."

The circuit doesn't average three numbers into one. It entangles three qubits onto a fourth and reads the prediction off how that fourth qubit collapses, over 8,192 shots on Qiskit's Aer simulator.

02The circuit

Three feature qubits, each rotated by its own Ry(θ), then chained onto a decision qubit through controlled rotations weighted by how much each feature should matter (rank 0.55, form 0.30, fatigue 0.15). All four qubits are measured; the fraction of shots where the decision qubit reads 1 is player A's win probability.

One circuit per matchup · run for 8,192 shots on Qiskit Aer · measured fraction of decision-qubit = 1 outcomes is the win probability.

03Grading it against reality

Predictions were made from Round 1 results only, before checking any Round 2 outcome. 04What's real here, and what isn't

Read before citing this anywhere

  • The circuit is real. Superposition and entanglement, executed on Qiskit's Aer simulator — not a classical formula with a quantum-sounding name.
  • But no quantum advantage is claimed. Because the feature qubits are only ever used as controls and nothing further acts on them afterward, the deferred-measurement principle means this circuit's output is mathematically equivalent to a classical probabilistic mixture over conditional rotations. It's a genuine quantum implementation of the feature-combination rule — it just doesn't need to be quantum to work this well.
  • 20 matches is a small sample. 80% from one partial round is an encouraging first read, not proof of a durable edge. Treat it accordingly until more rounds accumulate.
  • ATP rankings are a single stale snapshot (dated in data/atp_rankings.json), not a live feed — a player's real current form may have moved since.
  • Simulator only, and simple features. No real quantum hardware, and no head-to-head history, surface preference, or injury data factored in — just rank, Round 1 dominance, and fatigue.
